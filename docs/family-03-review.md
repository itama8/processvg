# Family 03 actuator reference review

Reviewed against local first-pass commit `86a568776ba41c25024a576640d4e267823b8b30`. The Family 01/02 audit at `0eed718e7fd46f1ae8611ecaeaf8e078bbb337fe` supplied the slide index and source limits. Its hydraulic changes are on a separate branch.

## Decisions

- **Mounting clearance.** On a flat host, an upright lever or float on a sideways-facing mount could start its transverse stem at zero outward reach. The stem then followed the host face and disappeared into that stroke. A reversed upright head reserved only one CSS pixel to the first transverse leg, which is the combined half-width of two one-pixel strokes. Sideways jogs now reserve two CSS pixels past the host strip, including the first transverse leg of a reversed return. This leaves one CSS pixel of nominal white space in the tested display profile. It is a drawing clearance, with the scale supplied by the host. The stem still starts at the declared mount and terminates at the head along its own axis. Mount-aligned placement, including flush cylinders, uses the existing rule.
- **Operator identity.** Keep the manual T, wheel, lever, gearbox, motor circle, solenoid box, diaphragm dome, P/H piston and float profiles. The external identifier still carries letters that no longer fit inside their heads at reduced size. The approved P and H identifiers distinguish utility medium on shared piston geometry. Output motion and control mode are separate declarations. The legacy diaphragm and modulating control-valve presets share a glyph. The catalogue and valve-function metadata carry that distinction. Specify control intent in the tag or detail where the sheet needs it.
- **Auxiliary connections.** The study circuit declares a separate three-way air pilot with its own S operator, IAS inlet, air outlet and exhaust. The pilot feeds the cylinder, the dashed electrical command goes to the pilot's S head, and distinct dashed feedback paths leave ZSO and ZSC. Solid stems represent physical linkage in these fixtures. The motor study uses separately labelled electrical power and command paths and a solid stem at the process valve. The forked ES supply follows the Pāhala P-001 example. The dashed command follows the existing Processvg electrical route grammar. These fixtures use explicit drawing-space endpoints and existing route styles. Operator, stem and spring graphic anchors carry no electrical or utility terminal semantics. Project instrument symbols, tagging, instrument air routing, power notation, terminal assignment and accessory topology require project input.
- **Valve state.** The air-pilot study uses the same valve declaration for the normally open design view and the closed operating snapshot at 0% reported travel. FC qualifies the separately configured loss-of-air response in both views. The operating snapshot gives no cause for the closed position. The motor example's FLP on loss of power is explicitly declared fixture data. A spring graphic identifies the return mechanism and supplies no failure direction. Equipment configuration and the cause-and-effect schedule determine responses to command, electrical supply and air failures.

## Integration and open criteria

Family 04 passes the same local cardinal mount, positive clearance where needed, placement and display arguments. Its channel faces and host-owned label placement are independent of the returned stem. An isolated copy of Family 04 commit `a10f64e8c245090bbf28328774092caa91082dce` with only the actuator implementation substituted was used for compatibility checks.

The base valve regulator has its own sensing tap and receiver and draws an impulse line. The companion Family 01/02 review routes that geometry through the shared `line-hydraulic` hook and short-run fallback. When the changes are integrated, reuse that route grammar if the project legend assigns it to the service and keep the process-pressure sensing role separate from hydraulic drive supply and control. The current actuator energy field provides no routing or endpoint declaration. Pneumatic route slashes also need enough run length to appear. On very short runs a service label or project detail carries the meaning.

Exact glyph compliance with licensed IS 3232 and ISA-5.1, a governing client legend, named project hardware, physical mounting and access, circuit topology, failure validation and dense full-sheet acceptance remain open. On the 65% copy, the reduced-size body is close to the line weight, and the external caption carries the small piston or motor identity. The reduced motor fixture uses the existing NC label option to keep the gate outline readable beside its flanges. The 300 dpi raster is a reproducible monochrome proof. Actual plotter, paper and copier behavior remain unverified.
