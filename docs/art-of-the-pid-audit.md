The deck uses refinery and petrochemical examples. Apply its review methods to wastewater drawings with the applicable client legend, equipment data and discipline review.

## Road map, legends, graphical grammar and identifiers

| Slides | Type | Convention or practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–4 | Practice | Treat the P&ID as the schematic record of functional connections. Equipment carries out process operations, instruments measure or control, and piping connects and conveys. Use the maintained drawing to define scope, specify systems, identify essential components, guide engineering and construction, track changes, support operator training and contribute to process safety information (PSI). |
| 5–8 | Practice | Give distinct equipment types their own symbols. Show inline and attached measuring and control devices, including local indication, analyzers, control valves, flowmeters, transmitters and software elements. Differentiate pipe and its fittings, valves, specialty items, insulation and tracing from instrument signals. Distinguish hardwired, pneumatic, hydraulic and software links with purpose-specific line patterns. |
| 9, 94 | Practice | Organize work around the governing legend, consistent sheet format, legible layout, content accuracy, staged completeness and cross-drawing consistency. |
| 11–13 | Practice / project choice | Settle the legend early and keep it synchronized with the drafting symbol library. Prefer the client's standard or an accepted precedent for that client. Obtain approval for a new project legend. The deck names PIP PIC001 as its default when criteria are needed. Agree at kickoff on symbology, layout, execution and change management. Use both a quality assurance matrix and a staged checklist. Cover abbreviations, equipment, instruments, piping, numbering keys and standard details in the legend. |
| 14 | Illustration | Decode shorthand on the legend, including service supply/return codes, battery limits, bypass and atmosphere, operational actions such as deenergize, and car-seal states such as CSC and CSO. The pictured list mixes tag, service and drafting vocabulary. |
| 15–16 | Illustration | Identify pump type and drive where useful. The examples distinguish motor-driven centrifugal and diaphragm pumps from pneumatic/hydraulic double-diaphragm equipment. Vessel outlines differentiate vertical skirt-mounted, horizontal leg-mounted, open-top, cone-roof and dome-roof arrangements. |
| 17 | Illustration | Distinguish an inline primary element from its associated transmitter. The legend contrasts a generic inline bubble, an integral FT, a separate FE and FT joined with a signal, an orifice and a magnetic flowmeter. Add the measurement principle where it clarifies the device. |
| 18 | Illustration | The sample signal legend uses dashed electric, open circles for software/data, paired slashes for pneumatic, repeating right-angle marks for hydraulic, crosses for capillary, a wave for electromagnetic/sonic/optical/nuclear, and filled dots for mechanical. Each pattern belongs to the sample legend. |
| 19 | Illustration | Distinguish flange, welded cap, concentric and eccentric reducers, union, hose connection, spacer, blind, and both orientations of a figure-eight blind. In the example, the open or filled element on the stem distinguishes spacer or blind and which side of the figure-eight is in service. |
| 20 | Illustration | Differentiate detonation arrestor, excess-flow valve with a flow limit, expansion joint, tubing filter, flame arrestor, flexible hose, injection/spray nozzle, inline static mixer and open drain. Retain the function-specific annotation where the legend calls for it. |
| 21 | Illustration | Mark normal position by fill for the pictured ball, diaphragm, gate and globe valves. The pictured butterfly and knife retain their identity glyph and add NC for normal closure. Differentiate swing/lift and spring-loaded checks, indicate allowed direction and show cracking pressure for the spring-loaded example (psig is its project unit). |
| 22–23 | Practice / project choice | Combine an equipment type code and unique identifier. The example groups letters by function and uses, among others, HE for shell-and-tube exchanger, K across blowers/fans/compressors, EJ for eductors/ejectors, P for pumps and TK for tanks. Adopt the project's equipment key. |
| 24–25 | Practice / project choice | Combine measured variable or function, instrument function/type and loop identifier. In the sample tables F, L and P on the variable side mean flow, level and pressure. T can denote temperature or transmitter depending on its position. C, E, I and V appear on the function/type side for controller, element, indicator and valve. |
| 26–27 | Practice / project choice | Define the line-number fields and their ordering with the client. Track size, service, sequence, pipe specification, insulation type and thickness, and heat-tracing requirement. Assign identifiers for specialty items outside the pipe spec and for tie points, samples and valve specs. |
| 28 | Illustration | A standard detail can pair a compact sheet representation with its expanded interpretation. The valve example shows an instrument-air supply, solenoid and fail-closed actuated valve, with the expanded detail spelling out command/indication and distinct open and closed feedback. Use the agreed detail to recover the omitted devices and connections. |

## Sheet format, routing and hierarchy

| Slides | Type | Convention or practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30–42 | Illustration / project choice | Lay out a sheet in repeatable zones. The example reserves a border, upper and lower pipe highways, incoming flags left and outgoing flags right, stationary equipment blocks near the top and rotating equipment blocks near the bottom. It puts notes and reference drawing numbers/titles in the right column and the revision log above the lower-right title block. The build-up places these anchors before equipment, then connects equipment, piping and loops in the final view. The final sheet uses pipe and signal styles, arrows, tags and alignments to help the eye follow dense detail. It pairs service and FROM/TO endpoints with P&ID references, uses a distinct utility reference form, twins parallel pump trains and includes cover-sheet/detail references. |
| 43–45 | Project choice | Agree on the client drafting procedure, sheet size, borders, title blocks, CAD layers, line weights and fonts. The deck illustrates ANSI D (22 × 34 in) and a half-linear-size ANSI B (11 × 17 in) reference copy. Leave a paper margin for copy misalignment, choose the border weight through the drafting procedure and place the filename outside the border in the illustrated convention. Assess graphical hierarchy and legibility at the issue and reference sizes. |
| 46–50 | Practice / project choice | Carry the client identity, location, subject, unique drawing number, revision, drafting and revision-description data in the title block. Slide 47 illustrates a revision table, approval/date fields, inch scale marks, drawing scale, company and filename. The sample subject hierarchy runs from plant/unit/project through drawing type and process section to the main equipment description. A sample number encodes sheet size, plant/unit/section, drawing type and sequence. |
| 54 | Practice | Choose meaningful titles and revise them as the sheet content changes. Maintain the revision log throughout development. |
| 56–58 | Practice / illustration | Show meaningful physical relationships on schematic drawings. The gravity-flow pair replaces a visual uphill lift with a descending connection by placing the source above the receiver. Check the actual elevations and service constraints separately. |
| 59–60 | Illustration | Make repeated branches easy to trace. The tank-to-pump example replaces scattered valve positions and out-of-order continuations with a common manifold, aligned branch valves, ordered destinations and clear arrow direction. The flags pair destination equipment with drawing numbers. |
| 61–62 | Illustration | Reorder vessel routes and reference positions to eliminate gratuitous crossings while keeping process connections apparent. The first drawing breaks lines at crossings to depict nonconnection. The second arranges the runs side by side. It moves a utility reference to the right to achieve that result, with its direction still explicit. The illustrations distinguish feed, utility, product, vent and relief and show an internal dip leg with a hidden line. |
| 63–64 | Practice / project choice | Start with the PFD and a provisional sheet list. Allocate equipment and reserve room for discipline additions. The deck's sizing heuristics are one column, one complex compressor or one fired heater per sheet, a complete package if it fits, one or two feed/storage vessels or tanks, one reflux vessel with its condenser, and pumps with related suction equipment. Adjust the division for complexity and reviewability. |
| 65–71 | Illustration | Compare a combined column system with separate column, reboiler and overhead sheets. Follow the off-sheet ties when splitting a system. The horizontal drum, vertical drum and storage tank sheets illustrate how orientation, equipment details, piping and loops consume space differently. The split examples show parallel equipment blocks, notes, revision tables and a revision-in-progress stamp. The vertical drum illustrates revision clouds with numbered triangles. The tank sheet shows a construction issue stamp and paired pumps. |

## Issues, completeness and consistency

| Slides | Type | Convention or practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 51–53 | Practice / project choice | Issue progressively more complete drawings. The sample sequence is preliminary/client comment (IFCC), approval (IFA), design (IFD), construction (IFC), and record (IFR). New drawings use letters for preconstruction, zero at IFC and sequential numbers thereafter. Revisions to existing drawings start with the next numeric revision plus a preconstruction letter suffix and drop the suffix at IFC. Use issue descriptions on new drawings and project title with issue stage on revised drawings in this example. |
| 84–85 | Practice / project choice | Check each issue against explicit stage criteria. Flag preliminary or unresolved information with HOLD clouds when appropriate. At preliminary issue, the example calls for completed legend sheets, equipment tags/titles, primary piping with size/service/spec, key process valves and primary loop elements and relief valves. |
| 86 | Project choice | For approval, add equipment details and blocks, secondary as well as primary piping, insulation and tracing, specialty items, all valve types, complete loops, motor controls, remaining relief valves and special notations. |
| 87 | Project choice | For design, complete design detail and line, specialty and tie-point numbering. Complete valve sizes/tags and instrument numbers. Show logic/interlocks, size all relief devices and carry visible holds on unresolved items. |
| 88 | Illustration / project choice | A staged equipment and piping matrix shows when a field first appears and is carried through later issues (inferred from first X and later O). Equipment count, names, configuration and spares start at preliminary; sizing, design conditions, driver, power, nozzles and insulation/tracing at approval; vendor design data and seals where needed at design; package details and vendor detail drawings at construction. Piping size/spec and arrows/continuations start at preliminary; secondary flows, spec breaks, insulation/tracing, samples, reducers and vent/sewer designations at approval; line numbers at design. The table excerpt ends at that row. |
| 90, 92 | Practice | Cross-check both ends of each continuation. Conduct process and interdisciplinary reviews under the QA matrix. When drawings have multiple authors, have the process lead review presentation regularly, peers check completeness and specialists review complex or specialty systems. |
| 91 | Illustration / project choice | The matrix connects inputs, outputs, review, approval and change control for each issue. Preliminary inputs include the legend and CADD layout criteria; subsequent inputs include prior drawings, discipline/client comments and eventually final vendor data. Use the completeness checklist for deliverables and back-check drafting pickups. The example moves from preliminary interdisciplinary review to formal discipline and client review at approval and follows up approval comments before design issue. Its visible gates include lead process and project engineer approval, then client approval at design and in the visible construction column. Early comments feed the next issue. After IFD, mark the master set, log design changes and incorporate them at the next issue. A PHA can use the IFD baseline or an updated special issue if held later. The visible text calls for formal management of change after PHA. The right and bottom edges of the matrix are clipped. |
| 93 | Practice | Identify required disciplines and obtain project/lead support. Schedule and size the meeting space, send review copies early, and bring supporting documents such as the line list and instrument index. Highlight reviewed components on a master markup set and plan follow-up sessions as needed. |
